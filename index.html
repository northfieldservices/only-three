An old idea

“Write down the most important things for tomorrow. Do the first one until it's done.”

As the story goes, in 1918 the steel magnate Charles Schwab asked a consultant named Ivy Lee how to get more done. Lee's advice took five minutes: each evening, write down the most important tasks for the next day, in order, and work through them one at a time.

Schwab tried it for three months, then reportedly sent Lee a check for $25,000.

Lee's list had six items. We use three, because most of us are lucky to finish six, and a list you finish beats a list you abandon.
